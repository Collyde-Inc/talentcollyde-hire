## Raw notes (taken during + immediately after)

**Background:** Jordan, late 30s, Portland-based. 8 years in wine — started as a server at Le Pigeon, certified Court of Master Sommeliers Advanced, last 4 years as Director of Beverage Programs at Pacific Wine Company (regional distributor). Knows our producers — actually placed two of them in Portland accounts during her time as a buyer. Charming, well-spoken. I want to like her. Already do, honestly.

**Gates:**
- 5+ years ops leadership — borderline. She ran beverage programs at the distributor, which has operational elements (inventory, allocation, vendor management) but it's not fulfillment-side operations. She called it "operations" repeatedly. I pushed her on team size — managed 3 reps + worked with a 12-person warehouse team she didn't directly manage. Marking **untested** for "direct ops leadership" pending follow-up.
- Operational metric ownership — she named "case throughput per rep" and "wholesale margin per SKU." Couldn't name a fulfillment metric (returns %, inventory accuracy, on-time delivery) when I asked. **Marking failed-pending — she may have these from her wholesale side but didn't surface them.**
- Portland presence — confirmed. Lives in NE Portland. **Cleared.**

**Block 1 — Why this role:**
"I'm ready to build something I believe in instead of moving cases for someone else's vision." Good answer. Then she pivoted to the wines we carry. Spent 2 minutes on a Loire producer. I had to redirect.

**Block 2 — Operational Specificity (the forced-answer probe per the kit):**
*Q: Walk me through the last time your operation missed a target. What was the metric, what was the gap?*

She paused. Long. Then: "We missed Q4 case volume last year — we were under-allocated on a key Champagne grower. I worked with our wholesale team to reallocate inventory from a Tuscan partner we were over-stocked on."

I pushed: how much under-allocated, how much over-stocked, what did she do specifically.

She said "I don't have the numbers in my head, but it was significant. I think we recovered most of it."

I followed up: what was the systemic fix.

She said "Better relationships with the producers. We had been treating them transactionally and that hurt us in tight allocation years."

That answer is about relationships, not about operations. She did this twice more in the next 10 minutes — when asked operational questions, defaulted to producer-relationship language.

**Block 3 — Operational Judgment (decision-under-unfamiliar-conditions):**
*Q: How would you handle a 7% returns rate problem on a subscription product you didn't pick yourself?*

She talked through it OK. Mentioned auditing the box composition, looking at customer feedback by tier, considering a curation algorithm change. But she kept assuming the problem was about *which wines* — not about packaging, fulfillment errors, or expectation-setting in the marketing copy. I had to surface "what if it's a damage problem during shipping" myself. She caught up quickly when prompted, but didn't get there independently. Her instinct is to optimize the curation. Not the operation.

**Block 4 — Cultural Resonance:**
Strong. She named "say the hard thing" as her own value — told me about firing a wholesale account that was selling our producer's bottles in a sleeve at 30% above MAP. Cost her $40K in commission that year. She cried telling me. She MEANS it.

On "care about the producer" — she actually named two of our producers and walked me through their histories. (Of course she did. Director of beverage at a distributor — this is her superpower.) Took 4 minutes I should have re-directed.

**Block 5 — People Leadership:**
Managed 3 reps. Promoted one to senior. Let one go after 14 months — talked about it thoughtfully, named what she'd learned (she should have set the bar at month 3, not month 12). Hadn't built a team from scratch. Hadn't managed a fulfillment team.

**Block 6 — Founder Partnership:**
Asked her what she wouldn't tolerate from a founder. She said "someone who doesn't trust me." I pushed: name a non-negotiable with a past example. She gave me: "I left my last role because the CEO promised me a strategic seat and kept me in a tactical one." OK, that's real. But when I asked what she'd push back on me about today — she defaulted to wines we carry. *"I'd want input on the producers we add."*

**Their questions for me:**
3 questions about producers. 1 about company stage. 0 about operations, fulfillment, team, or how I'd manage her.

This is the signal. Brittany asked me about my customer-complaint process. Jordan asked me about Chablis.

**My gut:** I want to advance her. She's brilliant on wine. She'd be a partner I'd love working with. I keep coming back to "but she'd actually do the producer work I can't get to."
