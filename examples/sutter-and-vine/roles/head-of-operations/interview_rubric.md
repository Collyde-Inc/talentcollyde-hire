## Layer 1 — Pass/Fail Gates

A candidate must clear all three. A failed gate means the recommendation is **Pass** regardless of composite score.

### Gate 1 — Operations leadership track record
**Verify:** 5+ years operating ops, supply chain, fulfillment, or DTC operations at director level or above. Confirmed via direct walk-through of last two roles — company, scope, team size, what they personally owned.
**Source:** Founder, intake 2026-05-09, [02:14] — *"I want someone who has run an actual fulfillment operation, not someone who's read about one."*

### Gate 2 — Operational metric ownership
**Verify:** Has personally owned at least one of: fulfillment accuracy %, inventory turnover, returns rate, on-time-delivery %, or unit fulfillment cost. Cleared when the candidate can name the metric, the current state when they took it on, and where they moved it to.
**Source:** Founder, intake [03:48] — *"I want them to be able to tell me a number, then tell me a different number that's the one after they did the work."*

### Gate 3 — Portland-area presence
**Verify:** Currently lives in the Portland metro or relocating within 60 days. Hybrid role — 3 days/week onsite at the Sutter & Vine warehouse.
**Source:** Founder, intake [11:32] — *"I need them in the warehouse. The team needs to see them."*

## Layer 2 — Weighted Scoring Dimensions

Weights sum to 100%. Each dimension scored 1.0–5.0 to 0.1 precision. The composite carries to 0.1 — never rounded.

### Dimension 1 — Operational Specificity (25%)

**What this measures:** Can the candidate describe an operational failure they personally owned in unglamorous quantified specifics. The single highest-signal dimension for this role (see Observation 1).

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | Volunteers a specific operational miss they personally owned. Names the metric, the gap in numbers, the root cause they identified, and the specific change they made. Tells the story without prompting — they keep coming back to operations as the frame. |
| **3** | Describes a generic operational improvement. Has metrics but doesn't anchor them to a specific failure they owned. "We" language more than "I" language. |
| **1** | Cannot produce a quantified operational story. Defaults to industry/domain talk when asked for specifics. Talks about strategy or vision instead of execution. |

**Hard rule binding:** A candidate who scores < 4.0 here triggers a **Hold — re-interview on operations** even if composite passes. Per Observation 1, this dimension is the kit's interrupt on Maya's pattern.

### Dimension 2 — Operational Judgment (20%)

**What this measures:** How the candidate reasons through an operational decision outside their domain. Decoupled from Cultural Resonance per Observation 2.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | Walks through a decision they made in an unfamiliar domain. Names the framework they used, the data they sought, the trade-offs. Acknowledges what they didn't know and how they got smarter fast. Comfortable being a novice. |
| **3** | Can describe a decision but defaults to "experience told me" or "gut." When pushed on a domain they don't know, gets quiet or generic. |
| **1** | Treats all operational decisions as variations of what they've done before. Pattern-matches without examining the differences. Or: paralyzed without a familiar template. |

### Dimension 3 — Cultural Resonance (15%)

**What this measures:** Alignment with Sutter & Vine's stated values — care about the producer, keep our word, make less but make it right, say the hard thing. Read for stories that demonstrate the values, not language that performs them.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | Tells stories that map cleanly onto the values without being prompted on them. Has personal experience that demonstrates "say the hard thing" — a moment they delivered uncomfortable news with stakes. The values resonate as their own, not Sutter & Vine's. |
| **3** | Recognizes the values in conversation. Can connect them to general past behavior. No specific stories that map. |
| **1** | Values feel performed. Recycles language back. Cannot produce a moment behind any value, even when prompted. |

**Warning per Observation 2:** A high score here cannot compensate for a low Operational Judgment score. Aesthetic agreement is not the same as values agreement.

### Dimension 4 — People Leadership (15%)

**What this measures:** Track record managing fulfillment / ops team members, including direct reports. Vendor and partner management included.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | Has built or rebuilt a team. Names specific developmental wins (a report they promoted, an underperformer they let go thoughtfully). Stories include the human cost and what they learned. Vendor stories show they hold the line without burning relationships. |
| **3** | Has managed a team. Stories are about projects more than people. Few named individuals. Conflict stories are missing or rushed past. |
| **1** | Has managed a team in title only. Cannot name a development story or a difficult conversation. Or: managed peers, not direct reports, and treats it as equivalent. |

### Dimension 5 — Founder Partnership (15%)

**What this measures:** How the candidate would work with Maya specifically — communication cadence, escalation, autonomy, willingness to push back. Maya's tell (per `company.md`): "people who know what they will and won't do."

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | When asked what they wouldn't tolerate from a founder, can name it specifically with a past example. When asked what they need to run autonomously, names a concrete cadence (e.g., "weekly 1:1, monthly strategic review, 24hr ack on Slack"). Has pushed back on a founder before and can describe how it went. |
| **3** | Has worked with founders. Can describe the relationship in general terms. Doesn't name specific non-negotiables or has them only in the abstract. |
| **1** | Treats the founder relationship as an HR function. Hasn't pushed back on senior leadership or can't describe an example. Asks for clarity from above but doesn't offer it back. |

### Dimension 6 — Domain Awareness (10%)

**What this measures:** Understanding of the wine subscription business — DTC operations, regulatory complexity (interstate alcohol shipping, age verification), seasonal demand patterns, producer relationships.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| **5** | Has operated in alcohol, subscription DTC, or specialty food/beverage. Names the operational quirks of the space — three-tier system, age verification, state-by-state shipping. Treats wine as the substrate, not the story. |
| **3** | Adjacent experience (general DTC, perishables, specialty retail). Curious about the wine specifics; doesn't pretend to know them. |
| **1** | No DTC or alcohol experience. Treats domain awareness as the answer to operational questions ("because wine, we…") rather than as one factor among many. |

**Weight rationale:** Intentionally low (10%) per Observation 1. Domain fluency must not compensate for operational thinness. A 5 here cannot mask a 2 in Operational Specificity.

## Decision Rules

The recommendation derives from gates + composite + the Operational Specificity gate (per Observation 1).

| Outcome | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Advance — strong** | All 3 gates cleared. Composite ≥ 4.5. Operational Specificity ≥ 4.0. |
| **Advance — qualified** | All 3 gates cleared. Composite 4.0–4.4. Operational Specificity ≥ 4.0. |
| **Advance — close call** | All 3 gates cleared. Composite 3.5–3.9. Operational Specificity ≥ 4.0. Cite the closest tension in the rationale. |
| **Hold — re-interview on operations** | Gates cleared. Composite passes 3.0 minimum. **But Operational Specificity < 4.0.** Re-interview specifically on the missing operational signal before any advance decision. |
| **Hold — re-interview on the gaps** | IDQ weight ≥ 30% of total. |
| **Pass** | Any gate failed, composite < 3.0, or critical IDQ with no path to fill. |

## Hard rules applied

1. **Evidence first, score second.** Stage 1 (evidence brief) is complete before any number is assigned in Stage 2.
2. **Founder intent overrides JD.** Maya's stated priorities in the role intake recording win over generic JD language.
3. **Gates are gates, not dimensions.** Cleared gates don't earn points elsewhere.
4. **Insufficient Data — never estimate.** IDQ + redistribute pro-rata.
5. **0.1 precision.** Round numbers (X.0, X.5) require anchor justification. Composite carries to 0.1.
